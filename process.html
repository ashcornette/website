How I work

Every project follows the same structure. The scale changes. The steps don't.

the framework

Six sections, same order, every case study. A hiring manager reading two projects back-to-back should know where the research lives, where the outcome lives, and what I owned.

sectionwhat it answers
01: ProblemWhat was broken and why it mattered
02: ResearchWhat I learned and how I learned it
03: StrategyThe decision that shaped everything after it
04: SolutionWhat was designed and built, with visuals
05: OutcomesWhat changed, measured where possible
06: LessonsWhat I'd do differently next time
01: problem

Start with the situation, not the brief. What was happening before this project existed? Who was affected and how?

One sentence that frames the tension. The pull quote is the problem stated plainly.
Evidence A stat, a quote, or a data point that proves the problem is real, not assumed
Scope How big was this? One screen, one product, one organization?

Template elements: 1-2 paragraphs of context. One pull quote. Stats grid with evidence.

02: research

What did I do to understand the problem before designing anything? Methods, findings, and the moment the direction became clear.

methodwhat it told me
InterviewsWhat real users said about the current experience
Competitive analysisWhat exists, what's missing, where the gap is
Data / auditUsage numbers, content inventory, heuristic review
Community researchForums, subreddits, support threads: unfiltered signal

Template elements: Method/finding table. Key stats or survey results. The research insight that changed the direction.

Scales down: For brand or personal projects where formal research didn't happen, this section becomes "what I already knew and what I checked." Even one competitive scan or content audit counts. The point is showing that design decisions weren't guesses.

03: strategy

The single decision that shaped everything after it. Not a list of features. One strategic move, stated clearly.

State the strategy as a sentence. If it takes a paragraph, it's not clear enough yet.

Template elements: The strategic decision in one sentence (pull quote). 1-2 paragraphs explaining why this direction and not another. Optional: before/after comparison showing the shift in thinking.

04: solution

What was designed and built. This is the visual section. Screenshots, prototypes, flows, before/after comparisons.

Template elements:

  • Screens or artifacts, grouped by feature or flow
  • Figcaptions on every image
  • Before/after compare table if applicable
  • Interactive prototype embed if available
  • Brief text between visual groups explaining the design rationale

Visual formats available:

formatuse when
.screens2-up side by side
.screens.triple3-up (mobile screens, personas)
.proto-embedVideo or Figma prototype
.compareBefore/after or method/finding tables
.statsKey metrics in a grid
05: outcomes

What changed after the work shipped. Measurable where possible. Honest where it's not.

project typeoutcome format
Shipped productStats grid: timeline, features shipped, platform, accessibility, monetization
Concept / studentCompare table: before vs. after. Projected impact. What would need to be true to validate.
Client workDeliverables list. Client quote if available. Before/after comparison.
Personal / brandScope and duration. What was built and what it replaced. Numbers if they exist.

Template elements: Compare table or stats grid. Testimonial blockquote if available. No invented metrics.

06: lessons

What would I do differently? What surprised me? What did the project teach that I've carried forward?

Template elements: 2-3 lessons, each as an h4 heading with a short paragraph. Honest, specific, not generic advice. The best lessons name a mistake or a surprise, not a platitude.

A lesson that names the specific thing, not "communication is important."
my role

Every case study ends with a clear statement of what I owned, what was shared, and what was inherited. This is non-negotiable for team projects and useful even for solo work.

What I owned The specific deliverables, decisions, and executions that were mine
Shared with Who else contributed and what they handled

For solo projects: State it plainly. "Solo: design, development, and shipping." The hiring manager still needs to read it to believe it.
